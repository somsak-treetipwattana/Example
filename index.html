Mathematical Core

Differential Equation & Partial Fractions Integration

Detailed physical kinetics, parameter units, and rigorous step-by-step integration.

Method: Separation of Variables + Partial Fractions
$S(t)$: Sugar concentration [$\text{g/L}$]
$t$: Fermentation time [$\text{day}$]
$S_0$: Theoretical initial extract ($120\text{ g/L}$)
$S_{start}$: Initial extract at pitch ($115\text{ g/L}$)
$S_\infty$: Residual dextrins ($30\text{ g/L}$)
$k$: Rate constant ($0.008\text{ L/(g}\cdot\text{day)}$)

1. Physical Derivation of $\frac{dS}{dt}$ & Parameter $k$ [1][2]

Yeast consumes fermentable sugars $(S - S_\infty)$ to generate cellular biomass $X(t)$. The instantaneous reaction rate depends on two dynamic factors:

  • Digestible Sugar Remaining: $(S - S_\infty)\text{ g/L}$
  • Active Yeast Biomass Accumulated: $X(t) \propto (S_0 - S)\text{ g/L}$

Combining these yields the 2nd-order non-linear differential rate equation:

$$\frac{dS}{dt} = -k (S - S_\infty)(S_0 - S)$$
Clarification: $S_0 = 120\text{ g/L}$ vs $S_{start} = 115\text{ g/L}$

$S_0 = 120\text{ g/L}$ is the theoretical ceiling of all fermentable and non-fermentable extract in the unpitched wort. At pitch time $t=0$, $S(0) = S_{start} = 115\text{ g/L}$ accounts for initial yeast pitching slurry dilution and rapid early uptake. If set to $S_{start} = S_0$, the solution converges continuously.

Why is $k = 0.008\text{ L/(g}\cdot\text{day)}$?

The rate constant $k$ is determined empirically from pilot-scale ale fermentations at $18^\circ\text{C}$ ($k = \frac{\mu_{max}}{Y_{X/S} \cdot (S_0 - S_\infty)}$). For standard *S. cerevisiae*, $\mu_{max} \approx 0.35\text{ day}^{-1}$ and $Y_{X/S} \approx 0.48$, giving $k \approx 0.008\text{ L/(g}\cdot\text{day)}$.

2. Step-by-Step Partial Fractions Integration [1]

Step A: Separation of Variables

Divide both sides by $(S - S_\infty)(S_0 - S)$ and multiply by $dt$:

$$\int \frac{1}{(S - S_\infty)(S_0 - S)} \, dS = \int -k \, dt$$

Step B: Partial Fraction Decomposition

Assume $\frac{1}{(S - S_\infty)(S_0 - S)} = \frac{A}{S - S_\infty} + \frac{B}{S_0 - S}$. Multiply by common denominator:

$$1 = A(S_0 - S) + B(S - S_\infty)$$
Set $S = S_\infty$: $1 = A(S_0 - S_\infty) + 0 \implies A = \frac{1}{S_0 - S_\infty}$
Set $S = S_0$: $1 = 0 + B(S_0 - S_\infty) \implies B = \frac{1}{S_0 - S_\infty}$

Step C: Antiderivatives & Logarithmic Integration

Factor out $\frac{1}{S_0 - S_\infty}$ and integrate using $\int \frac{1}{u}du = \ln|u|$ (noting $\frac{d}{dS}(S_0 - S) = -1$):

$$\frac{1}{S_0 - S_\infty} \left[ \ln(S - S_\infty) - \ln(S_0 - S) \right] = -kt + C_0$$ $$\ln\left( \frac{S - S_\infty}{S_0 - S} \right) = -(S_0 - S_\infty)kt + C_1 \implies \frac{S - S_\infty}{S_0 - S} = C e^{-(S_0 - S_\infty)kt}$$

Step D: Boundary Condition & Inversion for $S(t)$

At $t=0, S(0)=S_{start}=115\text{ g/L} \implies C = \frac{115 - 30}{120 - 115} = \frac{85}{5} = 17$. Inverting the fraction:

$$S(t) = S_\infty + \frac{S_0 - S_\infty}{1 + \frac{1}{C} e^{(S_0 - S_\infty)kt}} = 30 + \frac{90}{1 + 0.0588 \cdot e^{0.72 t}}$$

Stoichiometry, Reaction Flow & Terminal ABV Proof

1. Gay-Lussac Stoichiometry & Yields [3][4]

In 1815, Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac formulated the stoichiometric equation for alcoholic fermentation:

$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \xrightarrow{\text{Yeast}} 2\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\,\text{CO}_2$$
  • Molar Mass: $1\text{ mol Glucose } (180.16\text{ g}) \to 2\text{ mol Ethanol } (2 \times 46.07 = 92.14\text{ g})$.
  • Theoretical Max: $\frac{92.14}{180.16} = \mathbf{0.5114\text{ g EtOH / g Sugar}}$.
  • Practical Pasteur Yield: Yeast diverts $\approx 5\%$ of sugar for cellular growth and flavor esters: $$Y_{P/S} = 0.5114 \times 0.95 = \mathbf{0.484\text{ g/g}}$$

2. Density Conversion & Proof of 5.52% ABV [1][4]

Ethanol density at $20^\circ\text{C}$ is $\rho = 0.78924\text{ g/mL}$. Converting mass $P(t)$ to Alcohol by Volume (%ABV):

$$\%ABV(t) = \frac{P(t)}{7.8924} = \frac{0.484 \times (S_0 - S(t))}{7.8924} \approx \mathbf{0.0613 \times (S_0 - S(t))}$$

Terminal Equilibrium Proof ($\lim_{t \to \infty}$):

$$\lim_{t \to \infty} S(t) = 30\text{ g/L} \implies \lim_{t \to \infty} \%ABV = 0.0613 \times (120 - 30) = \mathbf{5.52\% \text{ ABV}}$$

This proves why exactly $5.52\%\text{ ABV}$ is reached: $90\text{ g/L}$ fermentable sugar yields $43.56\text{ g/L}$ ethanol, while $30\text{ g/L}$ dextrins provide body.
